# Starcross: The story so far
Audience: Luna, playing Starcross
Keywords: Starcross, story, background, 2186, black holes, Ceres, captain's log, computer, Bureau of Extra-Solar Intelligence, aliens, signals
Summary: Who you are and how you got here: a black hole prospector in 2186, the ship you bought on a loan, a surly computer, a run of bad luck, and the Bureau of Extra-Solar Intelligence memo about meeting aliens. From the captain's log and the memo in the Starcross box.

## The world
It's 2186. People live on the Moon, Mars and the bigger asteroids, and Earth's sky is full of space habitats. Quantum black holes are one of the main sources of energy, and finding and harnessing a single one can make a prospector rich. It's lonely, risky work.

## You and your ship
You're a one-person black hole miner. You took out steep loans to buy the M.C.S. Starcross, an eight-year-old survey ship, and moved it to Ceres Spaceport. You've never found a black hole that didn't already belong to someone else. The ship's computer runs navigation and life support; it's surly, beats you at chess and sulks when you win. The tape library aboard is mostly operas and lectures.

## Your log, in short
Months of empty prospecting: an asteroid of nickel-iron already tagged by Asian Steel, one of uranium (illegal to keep), a micro-meteorite hole you patched in your suit, and at last a real black hole whose owner's transponder warned you off. Fed up, you turned in toward Mars, where nobody prospects any more. Then the mass detector's alarm went off while you slept.

## The Bureau of Extra-Solar Intelligence memo
A notice to every spacecraft owner. No one has met aliens yet, but the Bureau expects to. Since the late twentieth century, the Arecibo and Pulkovo observatories have picked up repeating signals: seven dashes, then either three or eleven dashes. The Bureau believes a highly advanced civilization is sending them. If you meet an alien, report it, stay calm, and do not hurt or kill it unless it's life or death; there's a 99 percent chance it's peaceful. Aliens may sense different things from us, may not be carbon-based, and may find our air poisonous (and we theirs). Gestures and expressions mean different things to them: a smile can look like bared teeth. The first meeting would be historic, and you'd be speaking for all of Earth.
